Final Report — eBook Subscriber Insights (Phase 2)

Phase 2 of the eBook Subscriber Insights project scores all 5,455 subscribers on recency, logins and session minutes using approved thresholds and weights, splitting them into Active (55.3%), Inactive (28.0%) and Churned (16.6%) and setting a recommended strategy for each segment.

3 · Status mix

Active is the largest slice: 3,001 of the 5,422 classified subscribers (55.3%) are Active versus 16.6% Churned — the 33 Unclassified rows are excluded and shown separately. Association, not causation.

4 · Key findings

  1. Behaviour gap — Active vs Churned — Active subscribers average 11.69 logins and 29.14 session minutes per month; Churned average 1.54 logins and 14.15 minutes — a 7.6x gap in visits and a 2x gap in time. Last activity median: 5 days (Active) versus 152 days (Churned).
  2. Independent check — books and tenure (not score inputs) — The independent check that behaviour means something: Books Accessed and Subscription Tenure are not score inputs, yet books rise 4.1 → 9.1 → 20.2 from Low to High tier and tenure rises 16.7 → 19.9 → 20.6 months. A score built from recency, logins and session minutes still lines up with two inputs it never saw — that is the real validation.
  3. How big are the demographic effects? — honest reading — Two summary statements that frame everything: demographic effects are small (Cohen's d 0.20-0.33, Cramér's V 0.05-0.09 — practically modest even when significant) and behaviour carries the larger separation — with the honest caveat that the behavioural gap is partly by construction, because status is defined on recency and the score carries recency at 40%.
  4. High-value subscriber profile — In plain language: the typical high-value subscriber is a somewhat wealthier, slightly younger, married-with-kids household reader — earning about $99,810 a year versus $90,337 for churned subscribers (+10%), spending $94.1/month versus $83.5 (+13%), with 2.15 versus 1.74 children at home, at 62.2 versus 63.8 years of age.
  5. Null finding — genre does not separate statuses — Active rates span only 52.0% (Fiction) to 58.0% (Self Help) and churned rates 14.4% to 19.4% across the eight named genres (the small Unknown group, n=54, sits at 13.0%) — bands of about six points, with genre composition nearly identical per status (Fiction is about a fifth of every status: 18.5% of Active, 20.0% of Churned). Fantasy's apparent edge fails the Bonferroni bar (p≈0.037 > 0.0056), so no genre finding survives.
  6. Null finding — tenure does not separate churn — Tenure does not reliably separate churn. Churned rates by band are 16.5% (0-6 mo), 22.4% (7-12), 16.5% (13-24), 12.1% (25-48), 12.0% (49+) — non-monotone and overlapping; there is no clean "longer tenure = safer" story for churn.

7 · Recommended strategy per segment

Active — Retain

3,001 Active subscribers (55.3% of classified)
personalised recommendations
High-value = Active subscribers in the High score tier: 1,803 people, compared here with the 901 Churned.

Inactive — Win back before 90 days

Inactive (1,520 subscribers — 31-90 days since activity, median 58 days: the win-back window before day 90)
Trigger a re-engagement message the day a subscriber passes 30 days of silence
early warning signs
recency is the single strongest signal in the project (40% of the score; Active median 5 days vs Inactive median 58), and every Inactive subscriber is by definition inside the window — act at day 31, not day 80.
Send usage-based "pick up where you left off" nudges, not generic blasts
personalised recommendations
Inactive subscribers still average 3.31 logins and 20.1 session minutes — real residual habit, far above Churned (1.54 / 14.2) — so reminders built from their own recent reading volume and activity are the highest-probability conversion.
Prioritise the lower-Active segments inside Inactive
segment-specific strategies, relevant communication
Active rates lowest in Q1 income (51.0%), 1-child households (50.6%) and the older cohorts — all consistent with the surviving income/children/age effects (p<0.0056, though small) — so effort concentrates where Inactive is thickest, with messaging matched to each group rather than one campaign for all.

Churned — Win-back

Churned (901 subscribers — over 90 days, median 152)
Run win-back sequences keyed to declining login frequency
declining login frequency
Churned average 1.54 logins/month vs Active 11.69 — the decline is steep and long before day 90, so reopen contact using their historical reading pattern ("you used to read weekly") rather than a price offer.
Flag Low-tier subscribers approaching day 90 and intervene before churn happens
early warning signs
all 901 Churned sit in the Low tier because recency is 40% of the score and also defines Churned status, so this is not an independent finding. The practical point stands: a Low-tier subscriber entering the 31-90 day band is the pre-churn population to catch while still Inactive.
Give higher-value churned subscribers relevant, value-based reactivation
relevant communication, personalised recommendations
churned subscribers still average $90,337 income and $83.5 monthly spend, and the income/spend gaps to high-value survive correction (p<0.0056, small d) — the Q5 band alone holds 270 churned — so approach them with the value and quality of the reading experience they once used, not blanket discounts. (No recommendation rests on genre or tenure, per the null findings in (c).) ---

8 · Limitations

  • Association, not causation. Every relationship described here is co-movement in one dataset; nothing shows that acting on a factor will change status or score.
  • No cancellation reasons — we show who leaves, not why. The files record behaviour and demographics, never a reason for cancelling, so every churn statement describes when people stopped showing up, not what made them leave.
  • Status is recency-based by definition. Active/Inactive/Churned are thresholds on Days Since Last Activity (plus a ≥1-login guard for Active), so behavioural and score differences against status are partly built in — independent evidence comes only from inputs outside that definition (books, tenure, demographics).
  • No cancellation dates → no true retention curves. The tenure view is cross-sectional; it compares different people at one moment, never the same cohort over time.
  • Only the single most-read genre per subscriber is recorded, so content diversity, genre breadth and multi-genre reading behaviour cannot be tested at all.
  • The dataset is synthetic so conclusions illustrate method on this file — they are not market claims about real subscribers.
  • 33 Unclassified rows (missing or invalid recency) are never imputed for recency: they are excluded from tier charts and status shares, shown separately (32 Low / 0 Medium / 1 High tier) and scored only from logins + session with renormalised weights.

9 · Method in brief

  • Cleaning — approved rules C1–C10: drop 45 exact duplicate rows → 5,455 unique IDs; 26 raw spelling variants (23 after trimming spaces and ignoring case) collapse to these 8 canonical genres; invalid or missing recency never filled (→ 33 Unclassified); remaining behaviour blanks median-filled with an Imputed flag (D1: logins 6.00, session 21.80, books 9.00).
  • Score weights — 100 × (0.40 × inverted recency + 0.35 × logins + 0.25 × session minutes), inputs min-max normalised; books excluded; tiers by terciles (Low <= 40.80 < Medium <= 51.25 < High).
  • Status rules — Active = Days Since Last Activity ≤ 30 AND Avg Logins Per Month ≥ 1 — 3,001 subscribers; Inactive = 31-90 days since last activity, OR 30 days or fewer with fewer than 1 login per month (in practice 0 logins: 10 subscribers) — 1,520 subscribers; Churned = Days Since Last Activity > 90 — 901 subscribers; Unclassified = Days Since Last Activity missing or invalid (33 rows).
